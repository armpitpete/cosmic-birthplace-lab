Methods, accuracy and sources

This prototype deliberately separates physically calculated quantities from educational representations.

  • Planetary heliocentric positions: Keplerian method and elements published by NASA/JPL Solar System Dynamics. Tables covering 1800–2050 and 3000 BC–3000 AD; future dates beyond 2050 use the longer-range set. Positions are approximate, not precise JPL Horizons ephemerides.
  • Birth sky: simplified J2000 star catalogue and local sidereal time; planetary positions are approximate, Moon is a simplified orbital model. Omits proper motion, nutation, refraction and detailed atmospheric effects. It is an educational star chart, not a tool for navigation or telescope pointing.
  • Galactic motion: adopts ~220 km/s and ~230 million years per orbit (see NASA Solar System Facts). Motion assumes constant speed; spiral illustrations are not a survey of the Milky Way.
  • Light shell: distance c × elapsed time in the observer's simplified frame; the stellar examples are an illustrative subset. Real emissions spread and fade, and distances have uncertainty.
  • Gravity clocks: stationary weak-field gravitational comparison relative to distant observers, ignoring motion, nearby bodies and practical environmental conditions; black-hole example is a theoretical static Schwarzschild clock.
  • Cosmic expansion: educational low-redshift linear Hubble relation for large unbound distances and constant H₀ during a human lifetime; not a measured motion of a named galaxy. Solar System and Local Group are gravitationally bound and do not expand with Hubble flow.
